# StressLab Master Abstract

StressLab is an open-source discovery engine for systemic fragility in queue, flow, and dependency networks. We used it to run two linked studies aimed at testing whether structurally different systems share a common collapse-onset regime. The flagship study generated and stressed 5,000 synthetic systems across five topology families: random queue networks, scale-free networks, small-world networks, hierarchical supply networks, and market microstructure graphs. For each system, StressLab ran baseline simulation, minimum-shock failure search, worst-case search, collapse-metric extraction, and cross-system theory analysis. The result was a shared collapse transition band in baseline utilization around 0.10 to 0.19, with a median estimated threshold of 0.152 across families. Early-warning signals also generalized: collapse cases exhibited higher variance growth and longer recovery lag than stable cases. Cascade tails were heavy-tailed, but coarse model comparison favored lognormal fits over strict power laws. A controlled follow-up sweep over 2,700 fixed-size synthetic systems plus 144 healthcare variants showed that coupling only modestly shifts the threshold itself, with a mean high-versus-low coupling threshold shift of 0.008 utilization points, but reduces the shock margin to failure by about 39.2%. Together, the studies support a more precise claim: across domains, collapse thresholds are primarily utilization-led, while coupling mainly narrows the failure margin rather than moving the threshold much.
