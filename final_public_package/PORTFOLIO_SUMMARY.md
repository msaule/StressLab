# StressLab Portfolio Summary

StressLab is an open-source resilience and scientific discovery platform for queue, flow, and dependency networks. I built it to move beyond one-off scenario analysis and toward something closer to a lab for systemic fragility: generate large families of systems, stress them automatically, measure collapse dynamics, and search for reusable laws instead of just debugging isolated models.

The strongest result so far came from two linked studies. In a flagship campaign across 5,000 synthetic systems and five topology families, StressLab found a shared collapse transition band: collapse risk accelerates sharply once baseline utilization enters roughly the 0.10 to 0.19 range. Early-warning signals generalized too, especially variance growth and recovery lag. A controlled follow-up over 2,700 fixed-size synthetic systems plus 144 healthcare variants sharpened that result by showing that coupling only modestly shifts the threshold itself, but substantially reduces the shock margin to failure.

The main takeaway is simple and useful: across domains, collapse thresholds are primarily utilization-led, while coupling mainly narrows the failure margin rather than moving the threshold much. That gives StressLab a compelling research story, a strong demo narrative, and a practical resilience-planning message that is easy to communicate to technical leaders.
