# StressLab Master Executive Summary

StressLab is an open-source systemic fragility lab. It was used to run two connected studies: a 5,000-system flagship discovery campaign across five topology families, and a 2,700-system plus 144-healthcare-variant follow-up that isolated utilization and coupling in a controlled sweep.

The headline result is clear. Across domains, collapse thresholds are primarily utilization-led. In the flagship study, collapse risk accelerated sharply once baseline utilization entered a shared transition band around 0.10 to 0.19, with a median threshold of 0.152. Shared early-warning signals also emerged before failure, especially variance growth and recovery lag.

The follow-up sharpened the story. Higher coupling did not move the threshold much on average, but it substantially reduced shock margin. In the controlled synthetic sweep, the mean high-versus-low coupling threshold shift was only 0.008 utilization points, while higher coupling reduced the minimum shock-to-failure margin by about 39.2%.

That makes StressLab's main public claim both stronger and more precise: utilization is the main cross-domain collapse axis, and coupling mostly changes how forgiving the system is near the cliff. This gives the project a credible scientific narrative, a compelling portfolio story, and a strong foundation for a more formal publication.
