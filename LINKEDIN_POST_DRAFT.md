# LinkedIn Post Draft

I spent the last stretch turning StressLab into a research engine for systemic fragility, not just a simulation tool.

The most interesting result from the first flagship studies: across 5,000 synthetic systems and five topology families, collapse risk accelerated sharply once baseline utilization entered a relatively narrow transition band. A controlled follow-up then showed that coupling does matter, but mostly by shrinking the shock margin to failure rather than moving the threshold very far.

The short version is: across domains, collapse thresholds look primarily utilization-led.

That result held across random queue networks, scale-free networks, small-world networks, hierarchical supply networks, market microstructure graphs, and a healthcare validation family. StressLab now packages the whole workflow: synthetic generation, stress search, theory analysis, figures, writeups, and a public-facing narrative layer.

I like this project because it sits at the boundary of systems engineering, resilience research, and complex systems science. It feels less like building a simulator and more like building a lab.
